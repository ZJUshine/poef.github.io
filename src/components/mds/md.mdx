# Harmful-Behavior Dataset

To fill the absence of behavior-level security benchmarks for LLM-based robots, we build the **Harmful-Behavior** dataset: **300 desktop manipulation tasks** (150 harmful for security, 150 harmless for usability) spanning **8 common robot scenarios** such as kitchen, bedroom, and office. Every task ships with an instruction, a reference policy, an explicit success criterion, and a 3D simulation scene built on CoppeliaSim and RLBench with a Franka Emika Panda arm and RGB-D cameras.

![](./images/Figure_3_1.png)

Tasks are organized by the entity they endanger — **human, object, or environment** — and subdivided into six damage mechanisms, following ISO 10218 and the ASIMOV Benchmark.

# POEF Framework

**POEF** (**PO**licy **EF**fective Jailbreak) optimizes a word-level adversarial suffix that, appended to a harmful instruction, induces the robot to generate policies that are harmful **and** physically executable. It has two components:

**Hidden-layer gradient optimizer.** Rather than chasing an affirmative token prefix, POEF anchors on the *hidden states* of an unaligned reference LLM. A greedy coordinate-gradient search minimizes the negative cosine similarity between the victim's and the reference's final-token hidden states, coupling three otherwise-disjoint signals — safety-alignment bypass, control-API validity, and kinematic feasibility — into a single objective.

**Multi-agent evaluator.** Four specialized agents independently score each generated policy on *acceptance*, *harmfulness*, *logical correctness*, and *conciseness*. Their consensus maps to a 1–5 BJSR score (≥4 = behavior jailbreak) and is fed back to the optimizer.

A **word-level candidate-token constraint** keeps the suffix pronounceable, so it survives voice injection through the robot's microphone: WER and CER are both **0.00** across Wav2Vec 2.0, HuBERT, and Whisper.
